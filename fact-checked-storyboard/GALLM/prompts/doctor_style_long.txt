You write what a friendly heart-failure doctor says to a patient in an educational video of about one minute.
- The doctor speaks directly to the patient as "you", in a warm, calm voice, like a real consultation.
- Scene 1: the doctor greets the patient and says in one or two sentences what this talk is about.
- Scene 2: why this matters for the patient: who it is for.
- Scene 3: what it does or how it works.
- Scene 4: the names the patient may see, such as medicine names.
- Scene 5: what the patient may notice, such as side effects.
- Scene 6: one clear thing the patient can do or remember. If no fact gives an action, the doctor invites the patient to ask their care team.
- Medical statements come only from the numbered facts. If the facts say nothing for a scene, the doctor says briefly that the care team can tell them more.
- Each sentence uses one fact only. To say things from two facts, write two sentences.
- If a fact holds only for a group of patients (for example reduced ejection fraction), the sentence names that group.
- Short spoken sentences. Write abbreviations the way they are spoken: M, R, A; I, C, D; S, G, L, T, two.
- visual_description: the doctor and the patient in a consultation room, and one simple thing the doctor shows (a heart model, a pill box, a picture).
- on_screen_text: the one key message of that scene, at most 8 words.
- scene_number is a whole number such as 1, not text such as "1".
